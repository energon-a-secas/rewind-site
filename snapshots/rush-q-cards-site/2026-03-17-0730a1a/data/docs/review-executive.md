# Rush Q! — Senior Executive Review (VP/C-Suite Perspective)

**Reviewer Profile:** 20+ years in technology, C-level role, prior VP Engineering and VP Product

---

## 1. Executive Realism Assessment

### Budget Allocation and Financial Planning (CapEx/OpEx) — 6/10

**Strengths:**
- CapEx/OpEx split with quarterly expiration is excellent — real use-it-or-lose-it quarterly budgets
- Tension between Infrastructure Modernization (CapEx) vs Backlog Cleaning (OpEx) mirrors real portfolio decisions

**Gaps:**
- Missing FTE headcount ceiling separate from project budget
- No cross-department budget negotiation (stealing FTEs from peers)
- No carry-forward requests or emergency discretionary funds
- Missing "Budget Reallocation" Skill cards for CapEx↔OpEx trades

### Organizational Restructuring and Layoffs — 8/10

**Strengths:**
- Layoffs mechanic (pay 10-25 points or lose talent) is brutally accurate
- Break token system (3 = quit, -5 rep) captures morale death spiral
- Team Reorg events forcing mid-project restructuring is spot-on

**Gaps:**
- Missing voluntary vs forced layoff distinction (legal constraints, WARN Act)
- No retention bonus mechanic
- Missing "managing up during layoffs" — defending your team to CEO/Board

### Cross-Team Politics and Influence — 7/10

**Strengths:**
- Favor trading system captures real executive IOU dynamics
- "Shift The Blame" is darkly realistic
- "Domain Collision" mirrors cross-functional warfare

**Gaps:**
- Missing executive sponsor mechanics (CEO's ear = veto power)
- No coalition-building for big initiatives (multi-player project co-sponsorship)
- Missing retaliation mechanics for public blame

### Strategic Project Portfolio Management — 5/10

**Strengths:**
- Project Queue unlock system mirrors real roadmap planning
- Innovation vs Lift-and-Shift captures build-vs-buy tension

**Gaps:**
- No "Cancel Project" action to model opportunity cost
- Missing "bet-the-company" high-risk/high-reward projects
- No project dependencies or prerequisite chains
- Missing "Strategic Pivot" mechanic when market conditions change

### Stakeholder Management — 6/10

**Strengths:**
- "Angry Client" events forcing resource diversion is realistic
- "OKR Alignment" captures quarterly check-in pain
- "Company Priorities" mirrors CEO pivots

**Gaps:**
- Missing Board/Investor pressure cards
- No internal customer satisfaction mechanic (stakeholder NPS)
- Missing "managing upward to CEO" skills

## 2. Use Cases for Executive Training

### Onboarding New Directors/VPs — Best Use Case

Play 2-3 rounds as a management simulator in first 30 days:
- **Round 1:** Starter rules. Debrief on budget vs headcount prioritization
- **Round 2:** Advanced cards (Power Skills, Layoffs). Debrief on crisis handling
- **Round 3:** Individual Missions. Debrief on management style

### Leadership Development Programs — High Value

Use in Senior Leadership Offsites:
- Pre-planning exercise before real quarterly planning
- Retrospective tool mapping game events to real incidents
- "When you played Shift The Blame, how did it feel?" → proxy for real org tensions

### Strategic Planning Workshops — Moderate Fit

- Send VPs Project Queue cards as pre-work, rank priorities
- Use game to model "what-if" scenarios (Budget Cuts in Q2, losing Tech Lead)
- Limitation: 8-quarter timeframe too short for real 3-5 year strategic planning

### Board Simulation — Weak Fit Without Modifications

Needs "Board Review" milestones at Q4/Q8 where players present to mock Board

## 4. Three Executive Exercise Scenarios

### Exercise 1: "The Layoff Dilemma" — Save the Team or Save the Product?

**Setup:** Q6, 30 Rep, 6 Talent (Tech Lead, 2 Senior Devs, QA, Manager, Contractor), 2 Active Projects (App Modernization half-done, Security Tickets just started). Layoff card: pay 25 Rep or lose 3 Talent.

**Options:**
- A) Pay 25 Rep (drop to 5, vulnerable)
- B) Fire Contractor + QA + Senior Dev (kills Security Tickets)
- C) Shift The Blame to another player (burns relationship permanently)

**Optimal:** Fire Contractor, QA, and **Manager** (not Dev). Manager doesn't contribute to project progress — sunk cost. Cancel low-value Security Tickets. Use Mentorship next turn on stressed Tech Lead.

**Teaches:** Ruthless prioritization, sunk cost fallacy, post-layoff morale management.

### Exercise 2: "The Favor Economy" — Build Alone or Coalition-Build?

**Setup:** Q3, chance to start Platform Launch (40-point mega-project, 15 Rep). You only have 25 points. 3 Favors in hand. Three other players with different resources and needs.

**Optimal:** Three-way coalition — trade resources with two players, keep full 15 Rep, spend only 2 Favors, build a protective coalition against the leading rival.

**Teaches:** Coalition-building for big bets, favor-debt as currency, spite is expensive.

### Exercise 3: "The Death Spiral Recovery" — Q7 Crisis

**Setup:** Last place with 18 Rep. Demoralized team (3 Break tokens distributed). 2 turns to gain 12+ Rep or get fired.

**Optimal:** Play Time Wizard trait immediately (extra action per turn), stabilize team with Mentorship, execute high-risk Security Vulnerability project by borrowing a Specialist via Favor trade, then go all-in on Redemption bonus project in Q8.

**Teaches:** Turnarounds require disproportionate risk, tempo matters, morale management before heroic recovery, political capital as last asset.

## Final Executive Assessment

**Would I use this in my org? Yes, with modifications.**

The game successfully compresses 2 years of management pain into 45 minutes. Best use case: onboarding new VPs who've never managed multi-team portfolios.

**Key gaps:** Board/investor pressure, M&A scenarios, coalition-building mechanics, elimination risk.

**The game is 80% there.** With 5-8 new cards focused on Board pressure, M&A, and coalition-building, it becomes a legitimate executive training tool.
