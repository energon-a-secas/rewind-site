# Rush Q! — Senior Engineering Manager Review

**Reviewer Profile:** 12+ years managing teams of 15-40 engineers across multiple squads

---

## 1. Day-to-Day Realism Assessment

### Team Composition & Hiring — 7/10
- Tech Lead as force multiplier: realistic
- Contractor (can be sacrificed for Attrition): darkly accurate
- AI Companion (requires Senior, inconsistent output): forward-thinking
- **Missing:** Junior engineers (need mentoring, 0.5 progress), Staff+ engineers (architect/unblock), Engineering Manager card (manage managers)
- Manager card feels like a Recruiter, not a Manager. PMs don't reduce hiring costs — they manage scope/timelines

### Project Management & Delivery — 8/10
- Progress token accumulation captures sprint-over-sprint grind
- Project Queue variety reflects real roadmap diversity
- Rush Quarter mechanics are extremely real
- **Missing:** Project dependencies, sprint velocity concept, progress variance (estimates are wrong), technical debt accumulation

### Events/Disruptions — 6/10
- Team Conflict, Attrition, Meetings events are realistic
- **Missing:** 1:1 meetings, PIPs, promotion cycles, knowledge silos, burnout mechanics, interview load events, executive reviews

### Cross-Team Politics — 4/10 (Critical Gap)
- Game plays like managing an island. Real management at 15-40 people is constantly negotiating with peer managers
- Missing: Dependency Hell events, Executive Escalation, Resource Negotiation skills, Org Reshuffle events

### Budget & Resource Constraints — 5/10
- Draft budget cards are a start but lack core tension of constrained resources
- No runway mechanic, no headcount vs contractor trade-offs

### Performance Management — 3/10 (Biggest Gap)
- Break tokens model burnout but that's it
- Missing: 1:1s, performance reviews, PIPs, coaching/feedback, promotion decisions, team morale (collective, not individual)
- Performance management is 40% of a manager's job and barely represented

## 2. Training Use Cases

### New Manager Onboarding — High Value
- Play 2-3 rounds in first 30 days with debrief after each
- Add New Manager Starter Deck with training wheels
- Include Debrief Guide with reflection questions

### 1:1 Coaching Scenarios — Medium Value
- Use game as conversation starter: "You're in Q5 with 2 break tokens on your Senior Dev and a Rush Quarter hit. What do you do?"
- Create Coaching Scenario Pack with 10 pre-set board states

### Team Lead Promotion Readiness — High Value
- Observe candidates on: strategic thinking, people focus, collaboration, resilience
- Score on rubric (1-5 per category)
- 2-hour game reveals same patterns you'd see over 6 months

## 3. Gap Analysis — Missing Scenarios

### Critical Gaps (Must Add)

**1:1 Meetings:** "Remove 1 break token from a person. Cannot make progress this quarter."
**PIPs:** "Choose underperformer. Spend 1 Favor + 1 quarter. Next quarter: +2 progress or they leave."
**Technical Debt:** Rushing projects adds Debt tokens that slow future work.
**Sprint Velocity:** Declare expected progress. Hit exactly = +1 Rep. Miss by >50% = -1 Rep.
**Stakeholder Communication:** "VP wants update. Project >50% = +1 Rep. <50% = -1 Rep."
**Burnout:** Person with 2+ break tokens produces -1 progress (not just attrition risk).
**Promotion Decisions:** "Senior with 8+ cumulative progress wants promotion. Promote (1 Favor, +2 progress) or decline (+2 break tokens)."
**Knowledge Silos:** "Specialist leaves. Lose all progress on their project unless Documentation card played."
**Remote/Hybrid:** All talent starts with +1 break token. "Offsites" card removes 1 from everyone.

## 4. Five Exercise Scenarios

### Scenario 1: The Toxic Rockstar (Mid-Manager)

**Setup:** Q4, Punished Senior (reduces morale) delivering progress but 2 other Senior Devs at 2 break tokens each. Scope Creep Rush Quarter active. Need to deliver Project Alpha (5/8 progress).

**Dilemma:** Keep toxic senior and risk both S2s quitting, or fire them and accept missing deadline?

**Teaches:** Managing toxic high performers, short-term delivery vs long-term team health.

### Scenario 2: The Promotion Trap (Junior Manager)

**Setup:** Q3, Senior Dev S2 eligible for promotion (costs 1 Favor). Only 1 Favor left. Another manager offers Tech Lead trade for your Favor.

**Dilemma:** Promote (keep morale, lose Favor) vs trade for Tech Lead (gain capability, lose person)?

**Teaches:** Investing in people vs short-term gains, promotion timing, negotiation trade-offs.

### Scenario 3: The Dependency Disaster (Senior Manager)

**Setup:** Q6, Backend Rewrite (12/15) blocks Frontend Redesign (8/10). VP demands Frontend ships this quarter. Missing Deadline Rush Quarter active.

**Dilemma:** Ship Frontend anyway (fake integration, add debt), pause Frontend and finish Backend, or negotiate with another player?

**Teaches:** Managing dependencies, tech debt trade-offs, stakeholder negotiation.

### Scenario 4: The Attrition Spiral (Mid-Manager)

**Setup:** Q5, Senior Dev S4 just quit. Remaining team has accumulated break tokens. Two projects in flight. Mentorship and Hiring events triggered simultaneously.

**Dilemma:** Stabilize morale (Lighten the Mood) or hire replacement? Abandon one project?

**Teaches:** Recovering from attrition, morale management, project triage.

### Scenario 5: The Layoff Survival (Senior Manager)

**Setup:** Q7, 15 Rep, 7 Talent, 2 projects near completion. Layoff: pay 20 Rep or lose 3 people. Hand includes Skilled Execution, Owed Favors, Defensive Maneuver.

**Dilemma:** Rush-complete a project for Rep to pay layoff, negate layoff with Defensive Maneuver chain, or accept losses strategically?

**Teaches:** Crisis prioritization, resource triage, long-term vs short-term thinking.

## 5. Card Suggestions

### New Event Cards
| Card | Effect | Why |
|------|--------|-----|
| 1:1 Meetings | Remove 1 break token from everyone OR skip (everyone +1 break token) | Core management activity |
| PIP | Choose underperformer. Spend 1 Favor. Next Q: +2 progress or leave | Last-resort intervention |
| Knowledge Silo | Specialist leaves → lose all project progress. Negate with Documentation | Bus factor risk |
| Promotion Cycle | Senior wants promotion. Promote (1 Favor, become +2) or decline (+2 break) | Career growth expectations |
| Executive Review | VP wants status. Project >50% = +1 Rep. <50% = -1 Rep | Managing up |

### New Skill Cards
| Card | Effect | Why |
|------|--------|-----|
| Documentation | Next time someone leaves project, don't lose progress. Costs 1 progress | Knowledge transfer investment |
| Pair Programming | 2 people on 1 project = 1.5x progress + knowledge sharing | Speed vs resilience trade-off |
| Manage Up | Negate Executive Review event. Costs 1 Favor | Setting expectations, buying time |

### Card Modifications
| Card | Current | Should Be | Why |
|------|---------|-----------|-----|
| Project Manager | Eliminates a rush quarter | Reveal top Rush Q card, pay 1 Favor to negate | PMs see crises coming, not eliminate them |
| Cross-Team Dependency | Reduce project cost by 1 | +3 progress to project. Costs 1 Favor | Dependencies are coordination tax, not cost reducer |
| Manager | Recruit for free once | Reduce break tokens by 1/quarter, manage up to 8 people | Managers retain and scale, not recruit |

---

## Final Assessment

**Overall: 7/10 as Game, 5/10 as Training Tool**

**Strengths:** Captures chaos/unpredictability, models resource constraints, Contractor/Punished Senior/Attrition mechanics are excellent.

**Critical gaps for training:** No people management loop (1:1s, feedback, PIPs, promotions), no technical debt, too isolated (no cross-team dependencies), no skill development (people are static), too linear (progress is deterministic).

**Bottom line:** Rush Q! nails the chaos and resource constraints of engineering management. But to be truly useful for manager development, it needs the *people* side: 1:1s, feedback, career growth, morale management.
