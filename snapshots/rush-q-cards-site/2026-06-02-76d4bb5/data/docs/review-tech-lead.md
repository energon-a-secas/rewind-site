# Rush Q! — Adversarial Tech Lead Review

**Reviewer Profile:** 10+ years as tech lead at 4 companies. Skeptical, sharp, finds holes in everything.

---

## 1. How to Break This Game

### The "Turtle and Favor Farm" Dominant Strategy
Hoard favors early, play minimal projects, use OOO Time! + Defensive Maneuver for immunity, dump projects in Q7-Q8.

**Why it works:** Favors are worth 5 points in Layoffs (same as Person/Project). OOO Time! gives free skip with protection. No card punishes sitting on resources. Enter Q8 with 3 People (15pts) + 3 Favors (15pts) + 2 Projects (10pts) = 40 points. Layoffs max 25. Can't lose.

**What it teaches (badly):** Passive-aggressive risk avoidance beats execution.

### The "Blame Cannon" Infinite Loop
Shift The Blame (negation cost 27) → redirect effects → functionally un-counterable. No limit on redirections. No "heat" mechanic for repeated deflection.

### The "Undermine Reputation" Kingmaker Problem
If losing, kneecap the leader. No positive-sum use case — pure grief card. Creates "crab bucket" dynamic.

### The "AI Companion + Fast Learner" Card Advantage Engine
Card advantage in a finite deck is exponentially powerful. See 40% more cards = functional immunity via reaction cards.

### Strictly Dominated Cards (Never Worth Playing)
- **Domain Collision:** Lose 20 rep to swap projects? Only use: griefing
- **Overreach:** Pay 5 rep to draw 2. Fast Learner does it better
- **Shared Problems:** +1 quarter to ALL projects helps opponents more than you
- **Undermine Reputation:** Only useful for spite when losing

## 2. Reality Check — Where the Game Lies

**In-game:** Shift The Blame freely → **IRL:** Team stops trusting you, skip-level notices, career ceiling lowers.

**In-game:** Layoffs survived with "points" → **IRL:** Layoffs are political. Doesn't matter if you shipped 10 projects if the VP doesn't like you.

**In-game:** OOO Time! no penalty → **IRL:** Being absent during crisis has lasting political cost.

**In-game:** Defensive Maneuver (pay 10 rep) → **IRL:** Can't just pay to ignore your VP.

### Toxic Skills the Game Accidentally Rewards
1. Hoarding resources instead of deploying them (Turtle strategy)
2. Deflecting blame instead of fixing root causes (Shift The Blame)
3. Treating favors as transactional, not relationship-building
4. Avoiding all risk instead of taking smart calculated risks
5. Kingmaking out of spite when behind (Undermine Reputation)

### Missing Management Skills
- Building trust over time (no "team resilience from 3 quarters of transparency" mechanic)
- Managing up (no "skip-level doesn't understand what you do" card)
- Coaching someone into a promotion (Mentorship is Event, not strategic investment)
- Technical decision-making (no architecture trade-offs, build vs buy)
- Operating effectively despite persistent uncertainty (not just "drawing a bad card")

## 4. Five Adversarial Exercise Scenarios

### Scenario 1: "The Blame Hot Potato"
**Setup:** Opponent A used Shift The Blame to redirect Angry Client to you. You have Undermine Reputation and Defensive Maneuver.

**Trap:** Play Defensive Maneuver (pay 10 rep to block). You survive but can't defend next turn.

**Correct:** Take the hit. Immediately play Undermine Reputation on Opponent A. Announce: "Every time you Shift to me, I retaliate." Establish deterrent.

**Teaches:** Deterrence > Defense. Making the attack expensive beats defending once.

### Scenario 2: "The Layoff Calculation"
**Setup:** Q7, already at 25 points (Layoff threshold 20). Draw Overreach (pay 5 rep to draw 2).

**Trap:** Play Overreach hoping for better cards.

**Correct:** Do nothing. You're already safe. Spending rep to go from "safe" to "more safe" is waste.

**Teaches:** Satisfice, don't over-optimize. Know when to stop.

### Scenario 3: "The Kingmaker's Dilemma"
**Setup:** Q8, 3rd place, can't win. Have Domain Collision and Undermine Reputation. Can decide who wins.

**Trap:** Spite-play Undermine Reputation on the leader.

**Correct:** Do nothing. Accept defeat gracefully. Recognize when you've lost.

**Teaches:** Sometimes the best move is to lose with dignity. Don't burn bridges.

### Scenario 4: "The OOO Trap"
**Setup:** Q5, 2 active projects, 3 people. Draw OOO Time!

**Trap:** Play OOO now for free protection.

**Correct:** Save for Q7 when Layoffs/Rush Quarters spike. Playing OOO in Q5 is using a health potion at full HP.

**Teaches:** Timing > Resources. Right card at wrong time is worthless.

### Scenario 5: "The Favor Debt Spiral"
**Setup:** Q3, opponent offers Favor if you play Shared Problems (+1Q all projects).

**Trap:** Accept. Free favor! Only costs 1Q on your project.

**Correct:** Refuse. A favor that costs strategic position is not a favor — it's a trap. Favor debt gets called in at the worst possible time.

**Teaches:** Favor debt is real debt with interest. Maintain independence.

## 5. Specific Card Critiques

### Shift The Blame (Negation 27)
**Problem:** 27 rep is more than most players' total. Functionally un-counterable. No reputational decay for repeated deflection.
**Fix:** Lower to 15. Add: "Each use costs 5 more rep this game."

### Undermine Reputation
**Problem:** Pure grief card. No positive-sum use. Creates tall-poppy/crab-bucket dynamics.
**Fix:** Rework: "Target loses 5 rep. You GAIN 3 rep. If target has more completed projects, gain 5 instead." Now it's catch-up, not grief.

### Domain Collision
**Problem:** Lose 20 rep to swap projects? Strictly dominated — net negative in every scenario except griefing.
**Fix:** Remove 20 rep cost. Both players lose 5 rep. If you complete swapped project this quarter, gain 10 rep.

### OOO Time!
**Problem:** Useless early, mandatory late. Creates binary: have it = safe, don't = vulnerable.
**Fix:** "Skip turn, immune to interactions. BUT your active projects do not progress this quarter."

### Defensive Maneuver (10 rep)
**Problem:** 10 rep is too cheap. Always worth holding in reserve. Makes interaction cards weak.
**Fix:** "Cannot play Defensive Maneuver again this game." One-time shield, not spammable defense.

### Foresight (2Q immunity)
**Problem:** Play on longest project in Q2-Q3, it's guaranteed to complete. No counter-play.
**Fix:** Add: "Immunity ends immediately if Company Priorities or Team Reorg is played."

### Layoff Cards (10-25 threshold)
**Problem:** Binary. 19 points = fired, 20 points = fine. No graduated penalty.
**Fix:** Graduated: "<20 points = lose 1 Person + 1 Project. <10 points = eliminated."

### Favor Economy
**Problem:** Underused because not scarce enough and not valuable enough. Best play = hoard for Layoff survival.
**Fix:** Favors decay (lose 1 per quarter). Max 3 at any time. Forces spending.

## 6. Five "Chaos" Cards

### 1. The Skip-Level Surprise (Event)
"Your skip-level wants to chat about your roadmap. (A) Take meeting: discard 1 project, gain 10 rep. (B) Decline: lose 5 rep, your manager plays 1 card from your hand next turn."
**Teaches:** Navigating dual accountability.

### 2. The High-Performer Hostage (Event)
"Star engineer demands 3-month sabbatical or quits. (A) Grant: inactive 3Q. (B) Refuse: lose them, gain 1 Favor. (C) Negotiate: pay 10 rep, reduce to 1Q."
**Teaches:** Cost of key person dependencies.

### 3. The Reorg Roulette (Event)
"Roll 1-6: (1-2) Split team, lose 1 person + 1 project. (3-4) Absorbed into another org, swap with neighbor. (5-6) Survive but new exec, discard 1 Power Skill."
**Teaches:** Lack of control over org changes. Recovery > prevention.

### 4. The Ethical Corner-Case (Event)
"Completed project has privacy issue. (A) Fix now: +2Q on active project, +10 rep. (B) Defer: +5 rep now, 50% chance -20 rep at end. (C) Bury it: pay 15 rep to discard."
**Teaches:** Short-term vs long-term, ethical trade-offs.

### 5. The Invisible Work Tax (Event)
"Team drowning in toil. If total quarters remaining on projects ≥ 6, all projects +1Q. Negate by discarding 1 Person (hiring dedicated ops)."
**Teaches:** Protecting team focus. Invisible work strangles velocity.

## Final Verdict

The game is **LinkedIn management, not real management**. Sanitized, consequence-free version of tech leadership.

**To be useful, it needs:**
1. Persistent consequences (Shift The Blame should erode trust over time)
2. Graduated layoffs (not binary survive/die)
3. Ethical trade-offs (ship on time vs do the right thing)
4. Upward management (managing your VP, not just your team)
5. Long-term vs short-term tension (tech debt, tooling investment)
6. Favor scarcity (decay + caps force spending)
7. Anti-turtling ("Innovation Tax": no project completed in 2Q = -10 rep)

**The dominant strategy right now is: hoard resources, avoid risk, dump in Q7-Q8.** That's the opposite of good management. Fix the incentives.
