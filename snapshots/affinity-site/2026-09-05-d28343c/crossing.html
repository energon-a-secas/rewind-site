Crossing corners

Nobody starts as a manager.

Which is a problem for any model that says your corner is fixed. The fix is to stop conflating two things: the corner your judgment came from, and the corner your job is in. The first moves slowly. The second moves whenever you change jobs.

Affinity

Where your effort converts cheapest.

Built from years of being wrong in one domain. It is not assigned at birth and nobody hands you a reading of it. You find out by trying things, which means the test can run twenty years late and still come back accurate.

Role

The corner the work sits in.

Moves freely, and should. A developer can become a PM, an SRE can become a manager, a designer can move into product. The model has never had anything to say about whether you are allowed to. It forecasts which half of the new job will feel cheap.

A developer who becomes a PM does not turn into a different type. They become a PM with engineering judgment, which is a specific and useful thing to be, and which predicts exactly where they will be strong and where they will struggle.

What AI changes

And, more usefully, what it does not.

AI is aura. Pouring more of it in does not improve the rate at which you convert effort into results in a corner. It gets you to the rate you already had, without the years of training that used to be the only way there.

Climbs fast

Output

Volume, polish, speed, and the number of things you can attempt in a week. An infra engineer handed AI and pointed at frontend work really can ship something, and pretending otherwise is how this model loses the argument. What arrives is fine, and usually more than the situation needed.

Never moves

The ceiling

Fine is not refined. The result is still a 40% result: a native would have caught the thing you cannot see, because that read is bought by being wrong in a domain many times. In your own corner you catch the bad suggestion. In someone else’s you ship it, at speed.

Expect AI to sharpen the corner someone already has, and to put the next corner over within reach. Do not expect it to turn a weak performer into a strong one. You get a faster climb to the same ceiling, and a more polished version of the same blind spot.
